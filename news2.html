Church leaders in Jerusalem condemn recent attacks in Taybeh

The patriarchs and heads of Churches in Jerusalem have issued a statement firmly condemning the “heinous attack” carried out on Saturday, October 17, by “masked settlers” in the Christian village of Taybeh. The attack targeted residents harvesting olives, as well as diplomats and officials from European countries who were visiting the area on a solidarity mission.

A widespread campaign of terror

“This wild attack on the Christian village of Taybeh and against its peaceful population is not an isolated incident,” the Church leaders stress in the statement published on the website of the Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem.

“In the past weeks,” they recall, “the Palestinian villages of the West Bank have been exposed to a wide-scale terror by settlers, which is reaching a peak during this time of the olive harvest, when families go to their lands to collect what they need to live and renew their generations-long bond with their lands.”

An unfounded religious ruling

In this regard, the patriarchs and heads of Churches in Jerusalem state that “the religious ruling recently published, allowing Jews to take the olives of Palestinians, as unfounded, very worrying and dangerous, and absolutely unacceptable.”

“We very much appreciate that several rabbis and Jewish voices have publicly rejected it,” the Church leaders note. They also thank Israeli President Isaac Herzog for his words of condemnation.

“Unfortunately,” they state, “the facts on the ground show that these attacks continue because of the impunity enjoyed by those who commit them.”

They acknowledge the intervention of Israeli police and the arrests made following the attack, expressing hope that law enforcement efforts will continue in a serious and consistent manner rather than remain an isolated measure.

Preserving the Christian presence

The statement concludes with an appeal to the relevant authorities to “bring those responsible to justice” and a call on the international community “to intensify its efforts to ensure the protection of the people of Taybeh and the West Bank, as well as all those who visit these areas to express their solidarity.”

“We renew our full support to the community of Taybeh and its churches,” the leaders declare. “As in the past, n the past, to do everything possible to preserve the Christian presence there.”

They conclude by offering prayers for the people of Taybeh and the entire population of the Holy Land, expressing the hope that they may live in conditions of security, justice and peace.

May I die only for you, Jesus, who willingly died for me

For two days now I have experienced a great desire to be a martyr and to endure all the torments the martyrs suffered.

Jesus, my Lord and Saviour, what can I give you in return for all the favours you have first conferred on me? I will take from your hand the cup of your sufferings and call on your name. I vow before your eternal Father and the Holy Spirit, before your most holy Mother and her most chaste spouse, before the angels, apostles and martyrs, before my blessed fathers Saint Ignatius and Saint Francis Xavier – in truth I vow to you, Jesus my Saviour, that as far as I have the strength I will never fail to accept the grace of martyrdom, if some day you in your infinite mercy should offer it to me, your most unworthy servant.

I bind myself in this way so that for the rest of my life I will have neither permission nor freedom to refuse opportunities of dying and shedding my blood for you, unless at a particular juncture I should consider it more suitable for your glory to act otherwise at that time. Further, I bind myself to this so that, on receiving the blow of death, I shall accept it from your hands with the fullest delight and joy of spirit. For this reason, my beloved Jesus, and because of the surging joy which moves me, here and now I offer my blood and body and life. May I die only for you, if you will grant me this grace, since you willingly died for me. Let me so live that you may grant me the gift of such a happy death. In this way, my God and Saviour, I will take from your hand the cup of your sufferings and call on your name: Jesus, Jesus, Jesus!

My God, it grieves me greatly that you are not known, that in this savage wilderness all have not been converted to you, that sin has not been driven from it. My God, even if all the brutal tortures which prisoners in this region must endure should fall on me, I offer myself most willingly to them and I alone shall suffer them all.

Bishop Mutsaerts on the Catholic Response to a (Synodal) Church in Crisis
Where Does Real Reform Begin?

Perhaps not in Rome.

Perhaps not at a synod.

Perhaps not at a bishops’ conference.

Perhaps it begins tomorrow morning.

When a priest spends another half hour in prayer before breakfast.

When a father prays the Rosary with his children.

When a young person goes to confession again after ten years.

When a bishop decides calmly to explain an unpopular truth.

When a seminarian chooses holiness over career.

When a parish begins Eucharistic adoration again.

When a Catholic teacher refuses to reduce the faith to vague humanism.

When a married couple truly lives marriage as a vocation.

That is how the Church has always been renewed.

Not primarily by programmes, but by saints.

So: no panic.

We may take the crisis seriously without making it more dramatic than it is.

Because the future of the Church does not ultimately depend upon the competence of her bureaucracy.

Thank God.

That may be the most consoling doctrine in this entire paper.

Christ did not say:

“You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build My five-year strategic plan.”

He said:

“I will build My Church.”

That does not remove our responsibility.

It puts it in its proper place.

We do not have to reinvent the Church.

Nor do we have to save her as though, after His Ascension, Christ somehow forgot how His own Church works.

We have to do something simpler.

Preserve the faith.

Speak the truth.

Receive the sacraments.

Raise our children.

Serve the poor.

Form priests.

Foster vocations.

Celebrate the liturgy with dignity.

Love our enemies.

And love Christ more than our own ecclesiastical faction.

That last one may be the most difficult of all.

Because in the end there are no traditional saints and progressive saints.

There are only saints.

And perhaps that is precisely the reform the Church needs now.

Not yet another new Catholic programme.

But Catholics once again taking the Gospel so seriously that other people begin to wonder:

“What have they found that we are missing?”

When that question is asked again, the new evangelisation will truly have begun.

And perhaps the Church will then rediscover something she has always known in her best moments:

The world is not converted because we increasingly resemble it.

It is converted because it sees something of Christ in us.

World leaders unite in prayer for Pope Leo

Prominent political and religious leaders from around the world expressed well wishes and prayers for Pope Leo XIV after he announced he would undergo surgery to remove a lung nodule.

The pope made the surprise announcement while addressing pilgrims Oct. 11 during his Sunday Angelus address. After doctors detected "a small, localized nodule" in his lung, Pope Leo said he would follow their advice and "undergo surgery to remove it." "The operation is already scheduled to take place in the coming days," he said. "I thank you for your spiritual closeness and entrust myself to your prayers."

Messages of support and prayers from around the world

Not long after his announcement, Italian President Sergio Mattarella published a letter to Pope Leo saying that the news of his surgery "was received with deep empathy and emotion by all the Italian people." "In view of your hospitalization in the coming days, may the affectionate thoughts of all Italians reach you, which I convey while adding my own personal sentiments," Mattarella wrote. "The Italian people stand close to you." The Italian head of state said the pope could count on the prayers of not only the Catholic faithful, but also "on the caring thoughts of countless people, in Italy as well as across the world, whether believers or nonbelievers."

Several world leaders also took to X to send well wishes to the pope, including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who wished Pope Leo "a successful surgery and a swift, full recovery." "Our thoughts and prayers are with him, and we hope he will soon be back in good health and strength," Zelenskyy wrote. "Ukraine is deeply grateful to His Holiness for his prayers and his strong, consistent calls for a dignified and durable peace."

In a brief message posted to X, Israeli President Isaac Herzog wrote: "From the holy city of Jerusalem, I wish Pope Leo XIV a full and speedy recovery."

Maltese Prime Minister Robert Abela also extended "prayers and best wishes" to the pope for "a successful procedure and a complete recovery."

Pope Leo also received prayers from Anglican Archbishop Sarah Mullally of Canterbury, who said she was "holding Pope Leo XIV in my prayers over these coming days, asking for God’s protection, closeness and care." "With Christians across the country and around the world, I pray for the pope’s swift recovery," the head of the Church of England wrote on X.

Viktor Eichner, the World Jewish Congress' representative to the Holy See, also posted on X, writing: "On behalf of the World Jewish Congress, I wish Pope Leo XIV a successful surgery and a speedy recovery. May he return to full strength soon."

Argentine President Javier Milei extended his "affection and best wishes" ahead of the pope's surgery. Pope Leo will visit Argentina Nov. 8-11. "All of Argentina accompanies His Holiness with affection and prayer, wishing him a prompt and full recovery," Milei wrote on X. "From our country, we join in prayer for his health, in the hope that he may soon continue guiding the Church with his characteristic wisdom and strength."

Questions about the Latin America upcoming trip

The surgery announcement came as Pope Leo prepares to embark on his longest international trip to date: a 12-day apostolic visit to Latin America, including stops in Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru.

At the Angelus, the pope greeted a group of Peruvian pilgrims and thanked them for praying for his upcoming visit, indicating the trip was still moving forward.

At a press conference in Uruguay, the first stop in the pope’s Nov. 6-17 apostolic trip, Cardinal Daniel Sturla of Montevideo told journalists Oct. 11 that although the announcement “was a surprise,” the pope’s greetings to the Peruvian pilgrims showed that “the plans remain the same.”

“Obviously, we are all praying for the pope’s health and trust that everything will go well, that he will recover and be able to be here on the scheduled date,” Cardinal Sturla said.

When asked if any precautions will be taken given that the pope is expected to arrive in less than a month, the Uruguayan cardinal said he believed so and that the pope “always travels accompanied by his personal physician and other healthcare staff.”

“I understand that some care will need to be taken,” Cardinal Sturla said. “We will be instructed in the coming days. We will be very attentive to that, but confident that everything will go well and that we are moving forward.”

EXCERPT EDWARD PENTIN SUBSTACK
Cardinal Burke Defends Title ‘Co-Redemptrix’ for the Virgin Mary

The Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of God, participated in an altogether unique way in the Mystery of Faith, the Mystery of the Redemptive Incarnation, and is rightly called Co-Redemptrix. So, too, to the degree that we unite our hearts, one with her Immaculate Heart, to the Most Sacred Heart of Jesus, we participate in the saving mission of Christ. We can rightly be called co-redeemers. In the words of Saint John Apostle and Evangelist, we are called to be in Christ “fellow workers in the truth.” Our Lord Himself makes clear the intimacy of our relationship with Him, when He declares: “If any man would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.” In addressing this International Conference on the Blessed Virgin Mary in Dogma and Worship, I want to present the unique share in the mission of Christ the King, the mission of the Redemptive Incarnation, which belongs to the Virgin Mother of God as Co-Redemptrix and our share in the same mission as co-redeemers.

The apparitions of the Virgin Mother of God at Tepeyac Hill in present-day Mexico City help us, in a particular way, to understand her mission and our corresponding mission. In fact, Our Lady’s children of Mexico, guadalupanos and guadalupanas, have always understood their mission in the world as service of Christ the King under the maternal care of His Mother whom He gave to us as our Mother as He was dying on the cross for our eternal salvation. Blessed Miguel Pro wrote two short prayers on the wall of the prison in which he was held before he was martyred on November 23, 1927, by a firing squad during the bloody persecution of the Church in Mexico: “¡Viva Cristo Rey! ¡Viva la Virgen de Guadalupe!” (Long live Christ the King! Long live the Virgen of Guadalupe!). So, too, Saint José Sanchez del Río, as he was cruelly tortured and killed, also by firing squad on February 10, 1928, repeatedly exclaimed: “¡Viva Cristo Rey! ¡Viva Santa Maria di Guadalupe!” (Long live Christ the King! Long live Holy Mary of Guadalupe!). Even as the Virgin Mother of God took the wine stewards at the Wedding Feast of Cana to her Divine Son for the remedy of their great woe, instructing them: “Do whatever he tells you,” so she accompanied these heroes of the faith to give their lives in doing God’s will. So, too, she draws our hearts to her Immaculate Heart leading us to give our hearts totally to the Sacred Heart of Jesus, even to the shedding of our blood with Him for the glory of God and the salvation of souls.

Regarding the Vatican and Our Lady as Co-Redemptrix -- these are the popes who promoted it:

Pius IX:
Ineffabilis Deus, 1854

Pope Leo XIII:
Encyclical Supremi apostolatus officio, 1883
Encyclical Jucunda semper, 1894
Apostolic constitution Ubi primum, 1898
Encyclical Adjutricem populi, 1895

Leo XIII’s encyclicals (including Jucunda semper / Iucunda semper of 1894, Supremi apostolatus officio, Adjutricem populi, and related texts) repeatedly describe Mary as associated with Christ in the work of salvation, offering her Son at the cross, and sharing in the procurement of human redemption. Some English renderings insert “co-Redemptress”; the underlying Latin describes the role without always employing that precise compound. Pius IX’s 1854 Ineffabilis Deus defines the Immaculate Conception in view of the merits of Christ and presents Mary as uniquely prepared for her place in the economy of salvation, but does not use the title “Co-Redemptrix.” Pius XII’s Mystici Corporis (1943) and Ad caeli Reginam (1954) describe her offering of her Son on Calvary and her association with the new Adam in redemption, again without always employing the exact title.

Pius X:
Encyclical Ad diem illum, 1904
During the pontificate of Pius X, a decree from the Holy Office on June 26, 1913, praised “the custom of adding after the name of Jesus that of His Mother, our Co-Redemptrix, the Blessed Virgin Mary.” The same congregation granted an indulgence for the recitation of the prayer in which Mary is called “co-redemptrix of the human race” on January 22, 1914.

Benedict XV:
Apostolic Letter Inter Sodalicia, March 22, 1918 states that Mary, suffering and nearly dying with her Son and offering him to divine justice insofar as it depended on her, can rightly be said to have “redeemed the human race together with Christ” (Ipsam cum Christo humanum genus redemisse).

Pius XI:
He is the first pope to use the term co-redemptrix. In his message to the pilgrims of Lourdes for the Jubilee of the Redemption, he made this prayer: “O Mother of piety and mercy, who as Co-Redemptrix stood by your most sweet Son suffering with Him when He consummated the redemption of the human race on the altar of the cross ... preserve in us, we beg, day by day, the precious fruits of the Redemption and of your compassion” (April 29, 1935).

In a 30 November 1933 address to pilgrims from Vicenza he said the Redeemer associated his Mother in the work and that “we invoke her under the title of Coredemptrix.” His 29 April 1935 radio message to Lourdes contains the prayer quoted in the post almost verbatim: “O Mother of piety and mercy, who as Co-Redemptrix stood by your most sweet Son suffering with Him when He consummated the redemption of the human race on the altar of the cross.” The 1923 letter Explorata res states that the sorrowful Virgin “shared in the work of redemption with Jesus Christ.”

Pius XII:
Encyclical Mystici Corporis (1943)
Encyclical Ad caeli Reginam (1954)

The 4 November 2025 Doctrinal Note Mater Populi Fidelis was issued by the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (signed by Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández and approved by Pope Leo XIV). The note states that the title “Co-redemptrix” “risks obscuring Christ’s unique salvific mediation and can therefore create confusion and an imbalance in the harmony of the truths of the Christian faith,” and that when an expression requires repeated explanations it becomes unhelpful.

Seven common myths and facts about the rosary

Here are seven common myths and facts about this devotion to Our Lady from Catholic News Agency:

1. Only Catholics can pray the rosary.

False. While rosaries are typically associated with Catholics, non-Catholics can certainly pray the rosary — and in fact, many credit it to their conversion. Even some Protestants recognize the rosary as a valid form of prayer.

2. Praying the rosary is idolatry.

False. Some have objections to the rosary, claiming it idolizes Mary and is overly repetitive. Just like any practice, the rosary can be abused — just as someone might idolize a particular pastor or priest, a form of worship, or fasting. But the rosary itself is not a form of idolatry. The rosary is not a prayer to Mary — it is a meditation on the life of Christ revealed in five mysteries “with the purposes of drawing the person praying deeper into reflecting on Christ’s joys, sacrifices, sufferings, and the glorious miracles of his life.” When we pray the Hail Mary, we are not adoring Mary, we are asking for her intercession — just as we might ask a friend or family member to pray for us. Second, any prayer can lose its meaning if we do not intentionally meditate on it. Focusing on the mysteries with purpose and intention is key to the rosary’s transforming power. As one author encourages: “The rosary itself stays the same, but we do not.”

3. You can wear a rosary as a necklace.

It depends. It is typically considered disrespectful and irreverent to wear a rosary around one’s neck as jewelry, even though the Church does not have an explicit declaration against doing so. However, Canon 1171 of the Code of Canon Law says that “sacred objects, set aside for divine worship by dedication or blessing, are to be treated with reverence. They are not to be made over to secular or inappropriate use, even though they may belong to private persons.” It is important to treat the rosary with respect and intention. If you intend to wear the rosary as a piece of jewelry, this would not be respectful and should be avoided. It goes without saying that wearing the rosary as a mockery or gang symbol would be a sin. But if it is your intention to use the rosary and be mindful of prayer, then it could be permissible. It is not uncommon in some cultures, like in Honduras and El Salvador, to see the rosary respectfully worn around the neck as a sign of devotion. Rosary rings or bracelets might be a better option if you want to keep your rosary close at hand as a reminder to pray, as they are kept more out of sight and would not be as easily misconstrued to be a piece of jewelry.

4. The rosary is an extremist symbol.

False. A widely-shared 2022 Atlantic article went viral for accusing the rosary of being an “extremist symbol.” “Just as the AR-15 rifle has become a sacred object for Christian nationalists in general, the rosary has acquired a militaristic meaning for radical-traditional (or ‘rad trad’) Catholics,” the article read. The author also cited the Church’s stance on traditional marriage and the sanctity of life as evidence of “extremism” and claimed that Catholics’ tendency to call the rosary a “weapon in the fight against evil” as dangerous. As CNA reported in 2022, popes have urged Catholics to pray the rosary since 1571 — often referring to the rosary as a prayer “weapon” and most powerful spiritual tool.

5. The rosary is not biblical.

Untrue! Most of its words come directly from Scripture. First, the Our Father is prayed. The words of the Our Father are those Christ taught his disciples to pray in Matthew 6:9–13. The Hail Mary also comes straight from the Bible. The first part, “Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with thee,” comes from Luke 1:28, and the second, “Blessed art thou amongst women and blessed is the fruit of thy womb,” is found in Luke 1:42. Finally, each of the decades prayed on the rosary symbolizes an event in the lives of Jesus and Mary. The decades are divided into four sets of mysteries: joyful, luminous, sorrowful, and glorious, the majority of which are found in Scripture.

6. A rosary bead, or pea, can kill you.

Somewhat true. A rosary pea, or abrus seed, is a vine plant native to India and parts of Asia. The seeds of the vine, which are red with black spots, are often used to make beaded jewelry — including rosaries. Rosary pea seeds contain a toxic substance called “abrin,” which is a naturally-occurring poison that can be fatal if ingested. However, it’s unlikely for someone to get abrin poisoning just from holding a rosary made from abrus seeds, as one would have to swallow them. Today, most rosaries are made from other nontoxic materials, such as olive wood or glass — eliminating this concern.

7. Carrying a rosary can protect you.

True. The rosary has proven to be a miraculous force for protecting those of faith and bestowing upon them extra graces, such as the victory of the Christian forces at the Battle of Lepanto after St. Pius V implored Western Christians to pray the rosary. Many great saints across history, including Pope John Paul II, Padre Pio, and Lucia of Fátima, have also recognized the rosary as the most powerful weapon in fighting the real spiritual battles we face in the world. We know that spiritual warfare is a real and present danger: “For our struggle is not with flesh and blood but with the principalities, with the powers, with the world rulers of this present darkness, with the evil spirits in the heavens” (Eph 6:11–12). “The rosary is a powerful weapon to put the demons to flight and to keep oneself from sin … If you desire peace in your hearts, in your homes, and in your country, assemble each evening to recite the rosary. Let not even one day pass without saying it, no matter how burdened you may be with many cares and labors,” Pope Pius XI said.

The Origin of First Friday Devotion

The First Friday Devotion is a beautiful practice within the Catholic Church that is focused on honoring the Sacred Heart of Jesus. The devotion dates back to the private revelations of Jesus to Saint Margaret Mary Alacoque, a French nun in 1673. In those apparitions, Jesus emphasized the importance of devotion to His Sacred Heart as a means of experiencing His love and mercy.

The main components of the First Friday Devotion include:
1. Receiving Holy Communion: On the first Friday of each month, Catholics are encouraged to receive the Holy Eucharist in a state of grace, meaning they should have recently confessed any mortal sins in the sacrament of reconciliation (confession).

2. Attending Mass: Ideally, Catholics should attend a special Mass held on First Fridays that is dedicated to the Sacred Heart of Jesus. However, if such a Mass is not available, attending any Mass on that day is sufficient.

3. Repentance and Confession: Along with receiving Holy Communion, Catholics are encouraged to make reparation for sins and offer prayers for the conversion of sinners.

4. Observing Nine Consecutive First Fridays: It is recommended that the faithful make this devotion for nine consecutive months as an act of reparation and to express their love and gratitude to Jesus for His sacrifice on the Cross.

5. Adoration and Prayer: Devotees are encouraged to spend time in adoration of the Blessed Sacrament or in personal prayer on First Fridays, meditating on the love and mercy of Jesus as represented by His Sacred Heart.

In 1674, Sr. Margaret’s spiritual director, Claude de la Colombiere helped promote the devotion, spreading it to others within the Church. As the devotion gained popularity, Pope Clement XIII granted a plenary indulgence for those who practiced it. Today, the devotion remains a cherished tradition for Catholics worldwide, reminding us of Christ’s boundless love and mercy.

While the devotion is not mandatory for every Catholic, it is a beautiful way to draw closer to the Sacred Heart of Jesus and to experience His love and mercy. The devotion also comes with some promises that Jesus made to Saint Margaret Mary Alacoque, including the promise of forgiveness of sins, deeper relationship with Jesus, and abundant graces for those who faithfully practice this devotion.

The Origin of First Saturday Devotion

The First Saturday Devotion was introduced by Our Lady of Fatima in 1917. During her apparitions to three shepherd children in Fatima, Portugal, Our Blessed Mother emphasized the importance of prayer and penance for the conversion of sinners.

On December 10, 1925, she appeared to Sister Lucia (one of the Fatima visionaries) and outlined the conditions for the First Saturday Devotion. She asked for the faithful to make reparation to her Immaculate Heart on the first Saturday of five consecutive months.

To fulfill the First Saturday Devotion, Catholics are asked to fulfil these conditions: -Confess and receive Holy Communion on the first Saturday. -Recite the Rosary. -Meditate for 15 minutes on the mysteries of the Rosary with the intention of making reparation.

Our Lady also promised to assist those who practice the First Saturday Devotion at the hour of death, with all the graces necessary for salvation. The First Saturday devotion is a powerful reminder of Mary’s maternal care and intercession. It serves as a way to grow in faith, deepen one's devotion to Mary, and seek spiritual renewal.

Today, the First Saturday Devotion continues to be practiced by Catholics worldwide and many people continue to receive countless graces from Mary through the devotion. The devotion is a testament to the enduring message of Fatima and the desire to respond to Our Lady's call for prayer and conversion. Our Lady of Fatima, pray for us!
