DAILY POST
3,083 Christians killed, 3,193 abducted in eight months

No fewer than 3,083 Christians were killed and 3,193 abducted in attacks allegedly carried out by Islamic jihadist groups across Nigeria between January and August 2026, the International Society for Civil Liberties and Rule of Law, InterSociety, has said.

The organization, in its latest report released in Enugu on Sunday, also alleged that at least 19,800 churches had been attacked or destroyed in Nigeria since July 2009, while 25 Catholic priests, pastors and other Christian clergy were killed, abducted or otherwise assaulted between January and August this year.

According to the report, July and August alone accounted for 437 Christian deaths and 393 abductions.

ACIAFRICA
Politicians Alone Cannot Fix Nigeria: Catholic Bishops Urge Citizens to Take Responsibility for Nation’s Future

Nigeria’s future will depend not only on political leaders but on the values citizens bring to public life, Catholic Bishops have said, urging Christians and young Nigerians to take responsibility for the society they seek to transform.

In separate interviews with ACI Africa on the sidelines of the media briefing marking the conclusion of the Second Plenary of the Catholic Bishops Conference of Nigeria (CBCN), the Catholic Bishops said that building a better Nigeria requires citizens to translate their faith and moral convictions into concrete actions.

Bishop Matthew Hassan Kukah of the Catholic Diocese of Sokoto called on Nigerians to reject violence and manipulation, respect others' dignity, and make responsible choices in the country’s political and social life.

“We Christians are expecting Nigerian politicians to fix Nigeria. No, Nigeria will be fixed as a result of the values that have shaped us as Christians,” Bishop Kukah said.

He said Christians must first examine themselves and ask how effectively they are living the values they profess, rather than placing the entire responsibility for transforming Nigeria on politicians.

“I think we must up our game because our country is sliding due to the shortage of values that kept Christianity in a good state. I think the first thing is for Christians to be able to look internally,” he said.
